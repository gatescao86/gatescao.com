I first encountered the mathematical concept of infinity in my calculus class. We learned something like 

$$
\lim_{x\rightarrow \infty} \frac{1}{x} = 0
$$

As a student, I accepted it without much thought. But what exactly is infinity? Is it an unimaginably large number? Or is it something fundamentally different?

For now, suppose *infinity* is just a very large number. Let's think of something big. Really big. As big as the universe. We know that the diameter of the observable universe is about 93 billion light-years, or $8.8 \times 10^{26}$ meters. Planck length, the smallest unit of measurement for distance, is $1.62 \times 10^{-35}$ meters. This means the observable universe is $5.4 \times 10^{61}$ in Planck length across. We also know that the universe is about 13.8 billion old. That's $4.35 \times 10^{17}$ in seconds. Planck time, the shortest unit of time, is $5.39 \times 10^{-44}$ seconds. That makes the universe $8 \times 10^{60}$ Planck times old. Moreover, the total number of atoms in the observable universe is estimated to be $10^{80}$. These are enormous numbers that represent the limit of physical realm under our current understanding.

Buckle up. We are just getting started.

Do you know where Google got its name? From a number known as Googol: the digit 1 followed by one hundred zeros, or $10^(100)$. Google’s headquarters, known as the Googleplex, takes its name from an even larger number known as Googolplex. 1 followed by Googol number of zeros, or $10^(10^100)$. Googolplex is unfathomably larger than the age, diameter or the number of atoms in the entire universe.
